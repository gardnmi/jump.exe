# The White Pill

A generic developer climbs through five fictional years of accepting AI coding.
The black pill at the bottom represents bitterness and giving up; the white pill
at the top represents curiosity, optimism and enjoying the ride. Their keyboard
comes along: accepting help does not remove their judgment or pleasure in making.
The character is original, with no real-person likeness, quotations or biography.

The world is their computer. Omarchy's Osaka Jade palette, square window borders,
terminal tabs, status lines and monospace text establish one visual identity.
Each window mixes physical hardware and software in a situation developers know.
There are no temples, castles, classical columns or fantasy landscapes.

## One conversation, then the environment

A caretaker sits on a PC case with a laptop, beside a task lamp and the black pill.
He explains the white pill and the long emotional detour. **E** advances or reopens
four short exchanges; leaving hides the conversation without stopping physics.
The original dialogue is in `story.py`. Later story comes from actual app content,
settings, hardware, familiar objects and increasingly changed habits.

| Year / stage | Three windows | Environmental story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 / Denial | My Code, My Keyboard · AI: Disabled · Just Autocomplete | Warm coffee, manuals and HAND MADE notes defend a comfortable routine. The editor saves repeatedly, a suggestion gets dismissed, a locked integration rejects incoming help, and autocomplete gets crossed out. |
| 02 / Anger | Build Failed · Fan at 100% · Revert Everything | Errors stack up, cooling blades spin past hot capacitors, warning LEDs flicker, and a printer feeds failed builds. Discarded fixes drift into a full wastebasket beside a wall of reversions. |
| 03 / Bargaining | Ask Every Time · Just the Tests · I Review Everything | Ruled scope grids, an oversized permission lock and stamped test sheets establish conditions. A permission cursor hesitates, test progress stalls, and every line still waits in a review queue. |
| 04 / Depression | Session Expired · I Used to Know This · Cursor at 3AM | An abandoned screen asks “anyone?”. Dust settles into empty switch sockets. Questions are typed and erased. A blinking 03:17 clock, cold mug and TOMORROW note share the room with a sleeping desk cat. |
| 05 / Acceptance | Pair Session · Tests Green · Enjoy the Ride | Human and agent take turns in neighboring terminals. A small terminal helper waves, packets flow through connected cables, checks pass, and plants return to the desk. The keyboard remains beside ENTER and the white pill. |

The app names, controls and source comments tell the story without a narrator.
The HUD marks the stages and fictional years. Hardware changes the silhouette and
movement: keycaps teach short jumps, cooling hardware contains a recovery basin,
a tall deletion panel supports rebounds, and disconnected keys permit long falls.
The last desk provides relief after the empty space.

The stages now change the room's dominant materials and light, not just its music
and accent color. Denial has a warm, worn personal desktop; Anger has riveted hot
metal, cracked displays and shredded changes; Bargaining has ruled scopes and
review sheets; Depression has unlit blue glass, missing switches and a lone lamp;
Acceptance has powered mint circuitry and paired previews of work taking shape.
`stage_scenes.py` draws these larger motifs and their different animation rhythms.
The desktop outside the game windows remains untouched.

## Motion

The player has idle, two walking, crouch, rising, falling, recoil and landing poses.
The caretaker sits, talks, looks up and points. The computer scenes animate their
own activities: a turning cooling fan, CPU meter, blinking cursor, git graph,
review gutter and cable packets. The lamp glows; fresh coffee steams, cold coffee
does not. Effects and props share the same native pixel grid as the terrain.

Movement also causes small events. A debugging duck startles off the first key;
bugs scurry out of a failed build; a landing spins up the cooling fan; a rebound
scatters deleted lines. A sleeping terminal briefly welcomes the player back.
The review cursor notices another arrival. Later a cool-colored duck flees a lost
shortcut, the last lamp brightens, and little Linux penguins greet the developer
near the shared workstation and final keyboard. These moments have distance gates
and cooldowns. They do not continuously retrigger while the player stands nearby.

A suggestion appears near the opening editor's upper shelf and gets rejected.
The late-night desk cat wakes as the player approaches; the terminal helper nods
and waves at the paired workstation. `world_details.py` gives each window its own
ambient cadence, from restless retries to hesitant reviews, long quiet pauses and
steady collaboration. Stalled progress uses small separated dots; notifications
avoid solid terrain so decorative activity does not hide landing edges.

## The summit payoff

Reaching the white pill starts a ten-second animation inside the actual summit.
The capsule floats into the developer's raised hand and dissolves into cyan light.
They crouch to press the giant ENTER key; its cap visibly depresses and a pulse
travels through the hardware. The terminal helper waves, a penguin hops, the first
duck flies past, and the caretaker returns. A quiet original chime accompanies
the pickup. The camera eases to frame the final window without changing the scale
or physical layout.

The developer puts down a keyboard beside a steaming coffee. In the summit's
terminal, the final exchange types out:

> you: Let's port Rust to assembly.
>
> agent: Absolutely. How hard could it be?
>
> [+] hand-assembling the borrow checker...
>
> estimated time: yes

The absurd new project carries into the completion card. The acceptance payoff
is renewed enthusiasm for making things, including spectacularly unnecessary ones.

The completion screen records active play time, jumps and the longest airborne
descent in native screen heights. Dev-assisted runs are marked as practice. The
statistics freeze at collection and survive replaying the ending during that run.
The scene reuses the game's original developer, caretaker, creatures and desk
props at the same native pixel density. The pickup plays on the real platforms;
only the result is a separate framed panel. Neither paints the surrounding desktop.

Movement pauses during the finale. Enter skips to the result after its first
second; a fresh Enter at the result returns to the summit. R starts another climb,
N starts with a new seed, and Escape exits. E on the completed summit replays the
ending. Music stays at the player's chosen volume; M also mutes the completion
chime. Losing focus pauses the story. After dismissing the ending, jumping and
falling work normally. The black pill remains a story object, and dev flight
alone does not collect the goal.
